Olympic Hill High-Rise Construction Project – Downtown LA

PROJECT OVERVIEW
================

Project Name: Olympic Hill High-Rise Construction Project
Location: Downtown Los Angeles
Owner & General Contractor: Onni Construction
Total Budget: $500 Million
Concrete Budget: $80 Million
Construction Timeline: 30 months

SITE SPECIFICATIONS
==================

Underground Parking: 7 levels
Parking Requirements:
- Bathrooms
- Ventilation systems
- Lighting infrastructure
- Full OSHA compliance

Reference Project: The Beaudry
- Height: 64 stories, 695 feet
- Type: Residential tower
- Challenges: Tight site conditions, financing issues (stalled)

SITE CHALLENGES
===============

1. Vandalism
   - High risk area
   - Requires enhanced site security measures

2. Traffic Congestion
   - Extreme traffic issues
   - Crypto.com Arena event days cause additional congestion
   - LA Convention Center events increase traffic
   - Night work feasibility must be evaluated

3. Limited Laydown Space
   - Constrains concrete sequencing
   - Affects material staging and storage

4. Weather Delays
   - Rain days must be planned and scheduled
   - Weather contingency required in timeline

5. Tight Site Conditions
   - May affect sunlight for outdoor amenity areas
   - Landscape integration over parking challenges

CONCRETE WORK & POUR SEQUENCE
==============================

Concrete Type: Self-climbing concrete

Pour Sequence:
1. Foundation (poured first)
2. Core walls (start early, pour floor by floor using self-climbing or jump forms)
3. Vertical walls & columns (poured after or simultaneously with core)
4. Floor slabs (poured after vertical elements, typically one floor behind core for curing)
5. Podium slabs & transfer girders (poured early but separately, longer cycle times)
6. Mechanical floors/architectural setbacks (custom sequencing as required)

Floor Cycle Time: 5 days per floor
Previous Best: 3 days per floor
Goal: Optimize to reduce cycle time

===============

2. Tight site conditions affecting design elements
3. Concrete pouring timing for:
   - Podium slabs
   - Transfer girders
   - Mechanical floors
   - Architectural setbacks
4. Permitting delays
5. Coordination issues with General Contractor
6. Vandalism and site security
7. Weather delays
8. Traffic-related delays

Critical Bottlenecks:
- Podium slab and transfer girder pours require careful timing
- Mechanical floor concrete work needs custom sequencing

BEST PRACTICES & LESSONS LEARNED
=================================

From The Beaudry Reference Project:
- Plan for tight site conditions from project start
- Secure financing commitments early
- Account for urban construction challenges
- Build in contingencies for 64-story residential towers

Operational Efficiency:
- Previous best floor cycle: 3 days
- Current target: 5 days per floor
- Opportunity: Optimize processes to reduce cycle time
- Equipment and curing schedules are key determinants

Communication Protocol:
- Maintain effective GC-to-subcontractor communication
- Coordinate permitting early and often
- Align on concrete sequencing plans
- Regular coordination meetings required
